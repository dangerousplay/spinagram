Real explanations mix kinds of diagram: *here is the system*, *here is what it decides*, *here is the life of
the thing it manages*. In spinagram any diagram can hold typed regions:

| Inside any `Diagram` | You get the verbs of |
|---|---|
| `with d.flowchart("Title") as fc:` | `Flowchart`: `start`, `process`, `decision` with `.yes()` / `.no()`, `io`, `end` |
| `with d.state_machine("Title") as sm:` | `StateMachine`: `state`, `initial`, `final`, `s.on(event, target)` |
| `with d.activity("Title") as act:` | `Activity`: `lane`, `sequence`, `parallel`, `action`, `send` |

Each region is laid out by its own engine (the layered one by default) and sits in the page like any box. Connect
across regions with the usual `a.to(b, "label")`.

## Tips

- **Pick the outer direction for the shape of the slide.** Tall regions (top-down flowcharts) sit side by side
  with `direction: "right"`; wide ones (activities flowing right) stack with `direction: "down"`.
- **Link to real nodes, not pseudostates.** An arrow into an initial node is rejected, so point at the first
  action or state instead.
- **Give each region a class** (`class_="flow"`) and style them as cards, so the reader sees where one diagram
  ends and the next begins.
- **Style cross-region arrows apart** (`class_="link"`, dashed and in an accent colour). They read as "this
  triggers that", not as part of either flow.
